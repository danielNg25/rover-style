# STYLE BIBLE: 2.5D explainer, from narration to original shots

Audience: shot-designer agents (sections 2, 3, 10) and builder agents (sections 4-9).
Units: 24 fps, 1920x1080. "f" means frames. Percent-per-second camera speeds are scale (push/pull) or frame width (truck).
Status words: MUST = reject if broken; DEFAULT = use unless the sheet states a reason; CALIBRATE = provisional number, to calibrate on the first episode (listed in 9.4).

---

## 1. What this style is and is not

### 1.1 What it is (all of this transfers)
1. Low-poly 3D sets, flat-shaded in 2-3 tones, no outlines; flat cut-out puppets standing on one plane; a real camera that translates through the set, so layers show parallax.
2. One clause of narration = one picture, about 2 s. The picture is literal first.
3. Few places, many angles: about 80% of shots reuse or redress a known set. Change is told by altering one variable of a place the viewer already knows.
4. Nothing is ever frozen: the camera drifts, every puppet idles, every set has loops, and something new happens at least every 1.5 s.
5. Expensive things are substituted, not animated: crowds, fights, walking, hand work, travel.
6. Text lives inside the world (signs, documents, screens, tags), never as a floating caption.
7. Dry humour: each paragraph ends on a small deflating button.

### 1.2 What it is not
- Not full character animation, not lip-sync, not a camera that whips, zooms or rotates.
- Not a metaphor film: symbols are at most 8% of shots.

### 1.3 Relationship to the reference channel (owner's decision, 2026-11-21)
The earlier ban on resembling the reference ("banned motifs", the obligation to differ in puppet look, label look and dressing, the identity gate) is REMOVED. The goal is to reach the reference's look and liveliness as closely as possible: large expressive eyes with heavy lids, thick brows, the lowercase name tag with a hand-drawn arrow, the same finish and lighting are all allowed and wanted. Use the exemplar bank as a direct model for style. (What story and which characters an episode shows is decided per episode by the owner.)

### 1.4 Channel identity (optional)
A channel may still fix its own palette families, tag wording (Vietnamese with full diacritics) and recurring devices for consistency between episodes, but nothing has to differ from the reference.

---|---|
| Proportion | Head:body about 1:2.2 (about 3.2 heads tall); visible neck and shoulders; children 1:1.6 |
| Face | Small separated eyes (dots or short ovals, each under 12% of head width); brow + eyelid rig; 8 mouth shapes; neutral closed mouth as default |
| Hands | Articulated, 3 base poses (open, point, grip) plus specials |
| Shape language | Chamfered straight-edge blocks, paper-cut edges; no smooth rounded "AI" strokes; roofline or silhouette motif taken from the topic's real architecture |
| Palette | 3 families per episode (home, opposition, abstract), 5 colours each, derived from the topic's real materials; one fixed state pair (bad/good) that is not red/green |
| Name tag | Vietnamese with full diacritics, uppercase or small caps, on a paper tag or stamp plate with a straight leader line and dot |
| Thought stage | One owned abstract stage (default: notebook page or folded-map tabletop where cut-outs stand up), entered by a page-turn |
| World-scale device | Flat paper map with spreading ink or pins |
| Attention/power marker | One owned effect (default: ink stroke or stamped seal) |
| No-counterpart device | MUST name at least one recurring device the reference has no equivalent for (default: the channel's mascot pair as on-screen button-gag vehicle). CALIBRATE which device after the identity gate |

Identity gate before episode 1: side-by-side still of our puppet, tag and thought stage; a reviewer must answer "could this be mistaken for the reference at thumbnail size?" with "no".

## 2. Sentence to shots

### 2.1 Pacing numbers
1. English narration: 3.25 words/s; seconds = words / 3.25. Vietnamese (VBee, 1.0 speed): 4.2 syllables/s (CALIBRATE from the first voiced chapter); seconds = syllables / 4.2.
2. Target 24-28 shots per finished minute; mean shot 2.1-2.5 s; 80% of shots between 1.2 and 3.2 s.
3. Shots per sentence: up to 8 words (12 syllables) = 1 shot; 9-16 words (13-24 syllables) = 2; 17+ words (25+ syllables) = 3. Always cut at a comma or conjunction.
4. Floor: 1.2 s. This is the studio's rule, not a reference fact. Exception: list beats may run 0.8-1.5 s, at most 4 in a row, never in the first 30 s.
5. First 30 s of a video (studio rule wins): every shot at least 2.5 s, at most 3 shots per sentence. A list there becomes one shot with one in-shot change per item.
6. Ceiling: no image unchanged for more than 3 s. A shot over 3 s MUST contain a hidden re-framing (DEFAULT: wide to medium, or back view to front, as a cut inside the same set-up) plus one more item from 6.5. A shot over 4 s needs two items plus the re-framing.
7. Cut lead: the cut lands 0.3-1.0 s before the anchor word; DEFAULT 10 f (0.4 s). CALIBRATE: this was observed, not counted. A cut after its anchor word is a fail.

### 2.2 Step 0: the paragraph pass (before any sentence)
1. List the paragraph's places, people and props.
2. Name the home hub (the set most clauses happen in).
3. Hub test: a place is a hub only if the script returns to it in 2 or more later paragraphs, or this paragraph holds 5 or more clauses there. Otherwise it is a one-shot backdrop.
4. Set budget for the paragraph: a new hub at most once per 40-60 s; one-shot backdrops are free inside lists and travel (up to 3 within 5 s); otherwise no new set.
5. Mark the button line (the narrator's aside or the last clause).
6. Mark reaction slots: one reaction per 6-8 shots.
7. Mark planted objects: any prop or document that will return later gets an insert now.

### 2.3 The procedure, per sentence
1. Split into clauses of 3-11 words (4-16 syllables). A clause under 3 words joins its neighbour. Connectives are never pictured.
2. Compute seconds. Over 3.5 s: split again or plan an in-shot state change.
3. Pick the anchor word by priority: (a) a new name; (b) a visible action verb with its object; (c) a concrete noun, role or place; (d) a feeling or verdict word, which becomes a face; (e) an abstraction, which becomes one prop.
4. Choose the mode from table 2.4. Tie-break: if a clause is both an action and a verdict, the action wins and the verdict is the second acting beat inside the same shot.
5. Choose the set: current set, then a hub redressed, then a library place, then new (within the paragraph budget). A new set opens on a wide.
6. Choose a framing different from the previous shot: wide for events, places, groups; medium for two-shots and gags; close for faces and names; insert for documents and objects. MUST NOT plan three consecutive shots at the same framing on the same set (allowed when the set changes, as in lists).
7. Cast as few puppets as possible. Use a back view for watching, walking and thinking. Any event that needs a feeling gets one bystander face.
8. Write the acting beats (section 6), one camera move, and the liveness list to the floor for that shot type (section 7).
9. Place the cut 10 f ahead of the anchor word; place in-shot events (tag, swap, face flip, stamp) on the word itself.
10. Fill the "differs from previous shot in" field. If you cannot name two differences (framing, set, cast, state), merge or redesign.

Framing mix to hit over a chapter: wide 40%, medium 35%, close 18-25%, insert and extreme close 4-5%. Extreme close is spent only on a face transformation. About 20% of shots use a back view; about 7% have no character; about 20% carry in-world text.

### 2.5 Sequence patterns (cite the id in the sheet)
- P1 Step in: literal wide, then literal medium on the same set (state the event, move closer).
- P2 Step out: literal medium, then literal wide (next event or place).
- P3 Fact, face, consequence: literal, reaction close on the same set, literal.
- P4 Two-shot exchange: medium, reverse medium (see 2.7).
- P5 Place jump: wide, wide on a different set (list item, scale jump).
- P6 Intro package: silent plant, tagged close on the spoken name, one demonstration shot.
- P7 Before/after: same set-up twice with exactly one variable changed, cut on the punchline word.
- P8 Match count: the same pose match-cut across N known backgrounds.
- P9 Depart, route, arrive.
- P10 Plant and return: insert now, the same object later with draw-on marks added.
- P11 Thought ladder: close with thought cue, owned stage, back to a tighter close; reuse the stage and escalate.
- P12 Button: held medium with one prop state or one look, then a set change.

### 2.6 Rules by content type (function first; the pictures are examples, invent your own)
1. List: one shot per item, cut on the noun, each on a different cheap or existing set. Two items = two objects in one display. "Dozens" = three groups of 3-4.
2. Number or count: counted physical objects, or an in-world chart with one highlighted bar, or a raised finger for "one". Never a floating numeral. Percentages = a filled container, a divided row of objects, or a coloured share of a map.
3. Date or era: a wall calendar, a dated sign, a stamp on a document, or the set's technology changing. One per time jump, 1-4 characters of text.
4. Money: stacks, crates, a ledger line with a draw-on circle, a till; growth = the stack rising in a held frame.
5. Organisation as actor: one building with its sign plus one spokesman puppet in the signature colour. Rivals = two buildings across one street or two stalls in one market.
6. Product: an object insert with one demonstrated use, then a bystander's face flip.
7. Rise and fall, market share: the same set redressed (queue length, shelf fill, lit windows, sign size); one variable per state.
8. Quoted speech: the speaker in a medium or close with mouth flaps; the quote itself is never on screen unless it exists as a document or sign (1-4 words).
9. Abstract idea: one prop in a real set first; the owned stage second.
10. Thought or hypothetical: P11. The stage may return, escalated; the same symbol device at most once per chapter unless it changes state.
11. Emotion: a close on the existing set with one face change. Two-part turns hold the framing and change only the face. Irony: the acting contradicts the words.
12. Time passing: the same set redressed (light, weather, props) plus one age or costume step on the puppet. No caption.
13. Travel: P9 with the map device, or a scrolling backdrop behind a held puppet.
14. Cause and effect: P7, or a match cut with another character swapped into the same pose.
15. World scale: the map device with one overlay, then 2-3 vignettes of 3-5 puppets.
16. Crowd: 3-8 full puppets, back views, mirrored groups; an army = one puppet instanced in rows, or tiny distant repeats; a conflict = two figures with different flags.
17. Violence and death: MUST NOT show choreography or a contact frame. Allowed: one wind-up or impact pose with a flash, then an aftermath image; an instant consequence (puff, object left behind, figure lying flat) in a held wide; a readable prop (marker, empty chair). Hot moments are shot from behind with onlookers reacting.
18. Negation or absence: show the almost-action (a hand lowers), the absence (an empty room, idle machines), or swap wrong for right inside one held shot.
19. Two alternatives: two states of one framing.
20. Repetition ("again and again"): P8, or the same confrontation moved across already-built sets.

### 2.7 Dialogue and confrontation template
1. Open on a wide that fixes the axis: A on the left looking right, B on the right looking left. They keep their sides for the whole scene; crossing the axis needs a wide that shows the move.
2. Coverage per hub: 4-5 positions (axial wide, two-shot, single on A, single on B, the object between them). A scene may run 20-30 shots on these.
3. Cut to whoever the sentence names; cut to the listener when the clause is a verdict.
4. A two-shot runs at most 2 consecutive shots or 4 s before a single.
5. A-B-A singles are allowed once, then break with the two-shot, an insert or an over-shoulder.
6. Return to the wide every 8-10 shots or on any state change.

### 2.8 Quotas and repetition guards
1. One reaction per 6-8 shots; one button per paragraph, followed by a set change.
2. The same acting sign (for example chin-stroke, point, shrug) at most once per 30 s.
3. The same camera position at most twice in any 5 shots (held-face pairs excepted).
4. The owned marker effect at most 2 per minute. "Watches a screen from behind" at most once per chapter.
5. Labelled introductions at most 0.6 per minute; if more names arrive, only people who return get a tag.

### 2.9 Introductions and callbacks
1. New named person (P6): plant small or from behind in a wide 1-3 shots earlier; centred close or medium on a clean background, tag draws on over 10-20 f on the spoken name; then one shot demonstrating the defining trait, ideally proven by a bystander's face flip. Unnamed roles get no tag.
2. New place: a 1.5-2.5 s wide with a tiny figure already doing something, then medium or close on the same set. Place names appear as signage or a map pin.
3. Callback: 1.2-3 s, original set, palette and framing, dropped on the time phrase. Every hub owns a hue so the callback is recognised by colour alone.
4. Planted documents return as evidence with only draw-on marks added.

### 2.10 The hook and the ending (functions, not a shot order)
Hook, first 20-30 s, 8-12 shots under rule 2.1.5. Use at least four of these functions in an order of your own:
1. Scale image: the largest-scale picture of the topic.
2. Withheld subject: the subject from behind, in silhouette, or as its effects.
3. Detail inserts: one or two defining objects.
4. Naming: the reveal lands on the spoken name with a tag.
5. Negation or contrast gag: the ordinary expectation swapped for the real thing in a held shot.
6. Scope: how far the subject reaches, as one shot with staged changes.
7. Quiet contradiction: a close that undercuts the grand claim and poses the question.
The hook uses one home set and at most 3 other places.

Ending, last 30-40 s. Use at least three:
1. One callback per chapter.
2. The thesis line as a single state change of the central object or face in a held frame.
3. Shots lengthen to about 3 s over the last 20 s.
4. Return to the opening image with one variable changed.
5. Final image: a topic-specific object left behind, slow pull-back, 4-5 s. This is the only near-empty long hold allowed in the film.

## 4. Set design

### 4.1 Two tiers
1. Hub: about 0.5 new hubs per minute (4-5 in an 8-10 min episode). Each returns at least 3 shots on first visit and at least 6 over the film, across at least 2 later paragraphs. Built with 5 camera positions and at least 3 redress states.
2. One-shot backdrop: one angle, one wide of 1-2.5 s, no dressing behind camera, single-plane low-poly kit. Up to 2.5 per minute, preferably from the library.

### 4.2 Functional set types and recipes
| Type | Function | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| Seat of power | Where decisions are made | Axial symmetry, floor lane, raised place for the decider, one emblem |
| Workplace | Where things are made or studied | One hero machine, bench or screen; a wall of readable diagrams |
| Home or small interior | Private feeling | Low ceiling, one window, one lamp, 3-4 personal props |
| Public place | Crowd verdicts, regime and mood changes | Open floor, 2-3 facades with signs, one landmark, cloth lines |
| Lookout | Thinking, chapter hinges | Railing or ledge foreground, deep view, back-view position first |
| Journey | Travel, exile | Path through 3-4 silhouette bands, weather |
| Abstract stage and map device | Thoughts, scale | The owned kit (1.4); 2 positions |
| Insert surface | Documents, objects | Table top, wall, board; shallow depth |

### 4.3 Depth layers
Exterior, back to front: (1) sky gradient with flat clouds and sun or moon; (2) 3-4 silhouette bands, each lighter and bluer with distance; (3) treeline or distant buildings with one landmark that says where we are; (4) mid-ground 3D pieces; (5) character plane on a ground with a painted path; (6) 1-2 foreground edge props, slightly soft, at about half the back-wall distance.
Interior: (1) back wall with window or screen, the brightest emissive element; (2) side walls or pillars; (3) floor with a painted lane; (4) step or platform; (5) hero prop; (6) foreground pillar or a character's back.

### 4.4 Prop counts
- Hub: 1 hero prop the set is named after, 5-8 secondary prop kinds, 3-4 declared ambient movers.
- One-shot: 3-5 prop kinds, 2 movers. A no-people panorama compensates with density (about 20 small elements in layers).

### 4.5 Light and palette
1. One motivated source inside the picture (fire, lamp, window, screen, sun); key from above-front-left; characters and props cast soft shadows and the set has light and shade areas (see 8c); NO halo on lamps or fires (see 8.2); a soft glow only on magic, light shafts and screens. Optional light shaft.
2. One dominant hue family covers about 70% of the area, desaturated; one accent; warm light points.
3. Puppets are 20-30% lighter and more saturated than the set; no puppet's signature colour equals its home set's hue.
4. Each hub owns a hue for the whole film.
5. Mood states: day (neutral key), dusk (warm rim, long shadows), night (cool grade, emissive windows), crisis (bad colour of the state pair), after (good colour, added growth or repair).

### 4.6 Re-dress methods
Change exactly one readable variable per state: sky texture; global grade; emissives on or off; weather particles; 1-3 swapped props; a changed sign or banner colour; damage (smoke, cracks); an added or removed structure; a different occupant.

### 4.7 Camera positions per hub
1. Establishing wide, tiny figure already acting. 2. Medium two-shot around the hero prop. 3. Single close on each principal, background sharp (optionally one blurred near-foreground edge object). 4. Over-shoulder or back view toward the thing looked at. 5. Prop insert. Optional 6: high or axial "small and alone" wide.

### 4.8 Set brief template
1. Name; type; tier. 2. Script lines served; expected shot count. 3. What must be read in 1 s (place, era, owner) and the landmark that says it. 4. Far background. 5. Mid-ground pieces (3-5). 6. Hero prop and where puppets stand. 7. Secondary prop kinds. 8. Foreground edge props. 9. Floor lane. 10. Light source, direction, emitters, shaft. 11. Palette family, accent, warm points; contrast check. 12. Ambient loops (hub 3-4, one-shot 2). 13. Redress states (hub 3+), one variable each. 14. Camera positions. 15. Text surfaces and wording. 16. Tiny establishing figure and action. 17. Reuse check: can an existing hub or library place be redressed instead? Default yes. 18. Banned-motif scan.

## 5. Characters

### 5.1 Template rules
1. One rig for everyone, built to the identity kit (1.4). No outlines, 2-3 tones.
2. Views: front, mirrored 3/4, and separate back art for every principal. Seated variant where the script needs it.
3. Recognition test: each principal is identifiable from behind at 20% of frame height by silhouette and colour.

### 5.2 Variation levers, in order of reading power
1. Silhouette (headgear, hair mass, body width, height, carried object). 2. One signature colour. 3. Hair or headgear shape. 4. One costume motif or emblem. 5. Material gimmick (silhouette with lit eyes, glowing, metallic), at most one per episode.

### 5.3 Required sets
- Expressions (brow + lid + mouth combinations): neutral, pleased, angry, sad, surprised, doubtful, smug, afraid. Each named support needs a default and one flip; principals need all eight.
- Mouths: 8 shapes (closed, small open, wide open, smile, frown, flat, "o", teeth-set), swapped 2-3 times per second while speaking, no lip-sync.
- Hands: open, point, grip, plus up to 2 specials per character (for example holding the signature prop).
- States over time: only as many as the script's time jumps require (typically 2-4 for a lead), one visual delta each.

### 5.4 Cast economics
About 1 new named or role puppet per minute; 5-8 named per 8-10 min episode; about 1.3 one-off extras per minute, drawn from 6 archetype bases that recolour into about 15. Per shot: 45% one puppet, 30% two, 15% three to six, 10% none.

### 5.5 Extras and crowds
Never more than 8 full puppets on screen. Options: mirrored groups of three; backs of 3-5 heads; a front row of 5-7 with dimmer copies receding; tiny distant repeats; one puppet instanced in rows that fade in by rank; dark silhouettes for hordes. Every extra still idles (7.1 G4).

### 5.6 Character brief template
1. Role; tier (principal, named support, archetype extra, silhouette or instance). 2. Story function in one verb; default expression and its flip. 3. Height relative to the lead; body width. 4. Silhouette feature. 5. Signature colour and accent, checked against home set and cast. 6. Hair, headgear, facial hair, glasses. 7. Motif or emblem. 8. Material gimmick. 9. Views needed. 10. Hands. 11. Expression and mouth sets. 12. States over time with deltas. 13. Introduction: tag wording (uppercase, diacritics), the shot it lands on, the demonstration shot. 14. Crowd use and maximum on screen. 15. Banned-motif scan.

## 6. Motion

### 6.1 Camera norms
1. Mix: push 35%, hold with drift 40%, truck or orbit 15%, pull 3-5%, follow or ride 2%.
2. Speeds: push or pull 1-3%/s at constant velocity; truck 1-3% of width per second, eased; hold = drift 0.5-1%/s. At most 5% of shots exceed 8%/s.
3. The move is running on frame 1 and still running at the cut. No whip pans or snap zooms. ("Translate, never rotate, constant speed" was too rigid: see 6.6.)
4. Choice: hold when the picture itself changes (swap, appear, before/after, gag); push on thought, resolve, reveal, and on inserts; truck on deep wides and walking; pull for aftermath, loneliness, scale; follow or ride for travel.
5. Shake: only on an impact, 2-6 f, on an otherwise held frame; about 1% of shots.

### 6.2 Acting: density and timing
1. Every shot has at least 1 acting or state change, and at least 1 per 1.5 s.
2. A speaking or featured puppet makes 1.5-2.5 pose or face changes per second, mouth included.
3. Superseded by 6.6: poses POP and hold; they are not eased over 8-12 f. Hands and whole arm shapes swap; cloth lags the body by 2-4 f.
4. Two puppets never change pose on the same frame, except as a synchronised gag.
5. Idle on every visible puppet: bob 2-4 px with a 0.5-1 s period (CALIBRATE: scale to 3-6 px at 1080p if it reads as static), head tilt about 3 degrees, pupil darts, irregular 4-5 f blink, cloth sway.

### 6.3 Acting vocabulary
Face flips (glare, scowl, smile, gasp, eye-dart); stand or sit base; back view; raise a hand; point or count; sag or slump; wave; think (hand to chin); arms crossed; shrug; lean in; recoil; look to camera.
Templates: two-beat (pose A on clause 1, flip on the key word); reaction close (one flip, or three poses for a realisation); held-face pair (same framing, only the face changes); covered-face character (acts with eyes, brows, hands, cloth).

### 6.4 What to fake
| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Walking | Glide with bob and hem flip; from behind, tiny in a wide, with a truck, or cut after 1-2 steps. No knees. Full-body profile walk over 1 s is a fail |
| Fighting | One pose, one flash, then aftermath; or flashes behind a calm foreground |
| Hand work | Prop glued to the hand; a tap with a spark; an insert of hand, stamp or pen; marks draw themselves |
| Transformation | Cross-dissolve or puff swap in a held frame. Appear and disappear is the cheapest strong action |
| Conversation | 2.7 |
| Vehicles and travel | Held vehicle with scrolling backdrop and speed streaks; route on the map device |

### 6.5 Keeping a long shot alive (over 3 s)
Hidden re-framing is mandatory (2.1.6). Add from: a chain of 3-4 face or pose changes timed to phrases; a prop conjured, grown or drawn on; a character entering or leaving; a continuous transformation of the set; a travelling background; a staggered group action.

### 6.6 Measured on a dialogue-and-effects scene of the reference (188-237 s), 2026-11-21 (the owner pointed at this scene; these numbers override 6.1-6.2 where they differ)
**Character frame rate is low on purpose.** After removing camera motion, in 10 of 19 shots every change inside the acting area happens on a 2-frame step (12 drawings per second), with some 4- and 6-frame holds, while the camera moves on every frame. Shots where something changes on every frame are effect shots (electricity, portal swirl), not character animation.
**Acting is pose-to-pose with pops, and dense.** In a 2-second two-shot (frames 5184-5230) the speaker goes through seven distinct gestures: hand to chin, hand lifting, pointing at the desk, back to chin, finger raised, palm-out shrug, open-hand wave, both palms up in defence. Each pose is held about 3-6 frames and the next one simply appears (at most one in-between); nothing glides. That is about 3.5 pose changes per second for a speaker. Arms and hands change SHAPE between poses (bent, straight, pointing), so a character needs a library of whole-arm and hand drawings, not only rotating joints. The head tilts and the torso leans with most poses.
**The listener acts too, on a slower clock:** arms stay crossed while the eyes go half-lidded stare, brows lower, a squint with a slight turn, one blink, then a glare: about one face change per 10-12 frames, each a pop.
**Eyes carry the face:** large eye whites whose SHAPE is cut by straight heavy lids (half-lid, squint, wide), pupils that jump, thick brows that change angle; mouth is a single short stroke that changes shape.
**Camera: light, compound, eased.** Moves are small and combine axes: a push with a sideways and vertical drift at the same time (shot at 225.9 s: +39% scale with a diagonal drift, fast at first and slowing to almost nothing: ease-out), a sideways truck with a slight pull-back (218.1 s), slow pedestal moves (about 25-33 px at 640 px width over 1.5-3 s), a slow roll or arc component of about 0.2-0.8 degrees over a shot (202.2 s), and paths that change direction inside a shot (209.8 s). So: plan camera paths as short curves in 3D (two or three axes, eased, often decelerating), not as one straight constant-speed line. Not verified: whether true orbits around the subject occur (needs a perspective analysis); one apparent 8-degree rotation was a missed cut.
**Effect scenes are layered and relight the room.** Machine switching on (225.9-233 s): (1) lamps around the ring light up one after another; (2) two glowing orbs with halos appear on the towers (glow IS used for energy); (3) jagged lightning arcs that change every frame; (4) a rotating swirl ring around a portal showing another place with its own flames and a figure; (5) loose papers blown around the room; (6) the whole room changes from dim warm brown to a pale green wash, on the walls, floor and character; (7) the character reacts (shocked face, arms out); (8) the camera pushes in, then cuts to an over-the-shoulder back view with the portal larger. An effect is never a single sprite: it is a sequence of these layers plus a light change plus a reaction.

## 7. Liveness contract

### 7.1 Global floors (every shot)
- G1. The camera transform changes on every frame: at least 1%/s push or truck; 0.5%/s is acceptable only in hold shots that contain two or more acting or state changes.
- G2. At least 3 independently moving elements (camera, each puppet, each ambient family count once); see 7.2 for more.
- G3. At least 1 acting or state change, and at least 1 per 1.5 s.
- G4. Every visible puppet idles, including extras and back views.
- G5. No span over 1.5 s without a new event.
- G6. At least 2 layers show parallax against each other.

### 7.2 Floors by shot type (one number set; this replaces all weaker wordings)
| Type | Camera | Ambient families (floor) | Total movers (floor) | Acting / other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide exterior | Push or truck with parallax | 3 from: cloth, sky mover, light, weather, foliage, tiny extras | 5 | A tiny or foreground figure doing something; 1 beat per puppet |
| Wide interior | Push along the axis or truck past a foreground edge | 3 from: flames (2+ instances), one mechanical or prop loop, cloth or banner sway, a background figure, a light state change | 5 | 1 beat per puppet |
| Medium, 1-3 puppets | Drift 0.5-2%/s | 2: background loops that the place explains (flame, cloth, machine, creature, weather) | 4 | Speaker 2+ beats; listener 1+ reaction |
| Close | Push 1-3%/s or hold | 1 in the (sharp) background | 3 | 1+ face flip; blink and pupil dart; a hand entering or shoulder move if over 1.5 s |
| Insert | Push or tilt, never static | 1 on or near the object (glint, glow pulse, flame, steam) | 3 | Text or screen: 1 draw-on, tick or scan event on the word |
| Abstract or map | Push or slow orbit | 4 | 5 | One continuous transformation carrying the clause |

### 7.3 Density floors by framing
Wide: at least 6 prop kinds and 3 depth bands. Medium: 3-4 prop kinds plus one foreground edge. Close: 1 live background element; the background stays sharp (see 8.1). Insert: the object plus 2 context props.

### 7.4 Ambient library by place
- Exterior day: cloth sway, 1-3 birds, cloud drift, tiny walking extras, foliage.
- Exterior night: lit windows (a few switching on or off as events), flat lamp shapes, sparse constant stars, insects or bats, fog bands.
- Interior: 2-4 flames, a window light shaft, cloth or banner sway, one mechanical loop. No generic dust motes (see 8.4).
- Workplace: blinking panel, gauge needle, steam, conveyor or fan.
- Weather: 40+ particles in 2 depth layers; replaces motes.
- Abstract or map: paper flutter, ink spread, pin wobble, drifting cut-outs, light pulse.

### 7.5 Effects library (all flat vector shapes)
Pop-in/pop-out with puff; owned marker (ink or seal); impact star with shake; draw-on marks (circle, tick, strike, dotted route); name tag draw-on; light or colour state swap; ribbon or beam between two points; expanding ring; smoke or steam circles (4-8); sign swap with chips; speed streaks with scrolling backdrop; shadow wipe; falling dust; glint sweep; silhouette crowd; instanced-crowd fade; face cross-dissolve (at most 2 per episode).
Transitions: hard cuts. Soft transitions only to enter the thought stage (page-turn) or a travel montage, at most 1 per 4 minutes.

### 7.6 Fail conditions (any one rejects the shot)
Static camera with a static puppet; a wide with fewer than 3 ambient families; a named-character close with no face change; a full-body profile walk over 1 s; contact fighting; a cut after its anchor word; a shake without an impact; any frozen sprite; any banned motif.

## 8. Finish

**Corrected on 2026-11-20 against an independent frame-by-frame audit of the reference (`AUDIT.md`, cross-cutting items 1-4, 6, 7) and a direct look at four reference frames (`out/learn/dof-check.jpg`). Where an older report or another section of this file disagrees with this section, this section wins.** The earlier technique analysis over-stated blur, bloom, dust and smoothness; building to it made the picture look soft, hazy and "AI-like" next to the reference.

1. **Sharpness.** The picture is crisp flat vector from front to back in wides, mediums AND close-ups: the background of a close-up stays sharp. The only blur allowed is on a NEAR-foreground edge object that overlaps the frame edge (a branch, a pillar, a shoulder), 6-12 px at 1080p, used in roughly one close-up in three to give depth. No full-frame depth of field, no background blur, no soft-focus pass. Default `blur = 0`.
2. **Glow.** No bloom pass and no halo on lamps, lanterns, windows or fires: they are flat cream or yellow shapes. A soft glow is reserved for (a) magic or energy held by a character, (b) a visible light shaft through a window or between trees, (c) a screen in a dark room; one per shot at most, 15-30% opacity, and it must not lift the surrounding set toward white.
3. **Sparkle.** No generic four-point flare or glint sprites sprinkled on gold, stars or water. Stars are small crisp squares or diamonds at CONSTANT brightness, sparse (count them per shot design; a night sky behind a set has tens, not hundreds). A sparkle is an event (an object appears, a spell lands), drawn as flat shapes for 6-12 frames.
4. **Air.** No generic floating dust layer. Particles appear only when the place explains them: snow, rain, embers above a fire, sparks from an action, magic motes around a spell. Then they are few, crisp and small.
5. **Timing texture.** Characters move ON TWOS (a new drawing or pose value every 2 frames at 24 fps, every 2-3 frames at 30 fps) with one or two staged in-between poses for a large change; never a smooth per-frame glide and never a jump of more than about 15 px of the head or 25 px of a hand in a single frame without an in-between. Cameras stay smooth (on ones). Flames, smoke and water change shape every 2 frames as ONE continuous deforming mass (3 fat tongues for a fire), never by re-randomising many spikes each step, which strobes.
6. **Blinks and idles.** Blink only where it serves the beat (before a look, after a shock), not on a timer; an idle is a small held offset that changes on twos, not a continuous sine wobble.
7. **Colour.** 70/20/10 area rule (dominant, accent, warm points); distance = lighter and bluer by flat colour bands; no vignette stronger than about 5% at the corners; keep whole-frame brightness moderate (mean luma of a bright interior about 150 of 255, not 170+); whites are cream, never pure white floors.
8. **Shading.** (Revised 2026-11-21 after looking at reference close-ups; this replaces "no soft shading at all".) Sets and props: flat cut-paper shapes, 2-3 flat tones per object, no outlines. Characters: flat colour blocks PLUS one very soft form gradient on the head and hands (a few percent lighter toward the light, darker away), never airbrushed detail, never a 3D-render look. Shadows: see 8c.
8c. **Shadows (revised 2026-11-21 from twelve reference wides, `out/shadow-check.jpg`; this replaces "one blob shadow, no cast shadows").** The sets are lit as real 3D scenes and almost every shot shows it: (a) every standing character has a soft shadow on the ground at its feet, usually short and offset to the side away from the key light (clearly directional in bright interiors such as a lab; a small soft patch in dim scenes); a floating character has its shadow on the ground below it; (b) furniture and props cast soft shadows onto the floor and the wall behind them, and box faces turned away from the light are a tone darker; (c) floors and walls carry broad soft light and shade areas (a pool of light under a lamp or window, a darker far corner), and a character standing in a shaded area is darker there. Shadows are soft-edged, a darker tone of the surface they fall on (never black), and they move with the character. Only abstract, space and fire-lit hell-like scenes go without. A shot with characters and no ground shadows reads as stickers pasted on a backdrop and fails review.
8b. **Motivated light on characters.** When a character stands near a visible light source (fire, lantern, window, screen, magic), the side of the face and body that faces the source carries a soft warm (or source-coloured) rim, and the far side falls off darker and cooler; the direction must agree with where the source is in the wide shot of the same place. Seen in the reference: a boy beside a campfire has a light warm band along the fire-side edge of his face and jaw in the close-up, with the fire at lower left in the wide. The whole character is also tinted toward the scene's ambient colour (cool at night, warm indoors). This is a light ON the character, not a glow around the lamp: rule 2 still holds. Whether the reference flickers this light with the flame is not confirmed; if used, change it on twos by a small amount.
9. **Grain.** None by default. (If banding appears in large flat areas after encoding, add at most 1-2% static grain; decide on the first 10 s test.)
10. **Text and labels.** In-world only; 1-4 words; uppercase for signage; documents carry one title and 5-6 short lines of invented but plausible content; screens use pictograms, bars and ticks; Vietnamese always with full diacritics; minimum glyph height 3% of frame height when it must be read; a name tag is present from the first frame of its shot with its arrow already drawn or drawing on over 10-20 frames. Subtitles are a separate layer and never part of the design.

### 9.2 Starter library and build order (first 8-10 min episode)
1. Puppet template v1 with back view, sit, glide-walk and costume slot; 10 s turnaround test.
2. Liveness kit: camera presets (push, pull, truck, follow, hold, shake) and 10 loops (flame, smoke, particles in 3 skins, cloth sway, window lights switching, birds, motes, water shimmer, blinking lights, cloud drift).
3. Effect set A: pop with puff, owned marker, impact star, draw-on marks, name tag, state swap, ribbon, ring.
4. Two hubs, each with 5 positions and 3 states (day, night, after).
5. Lead in 3 states, 4 named puppets, 6 extras.
6. Owned thought stage and map device.
7. Insert templates: document page, sign or plate, chart or screen.
8. Four library places: public square, office or classroom, road or landscape, night exterior.
9. Effect set B as needed: silhouette crowd, instanced fade, scrolling travel backdrop, face cross-dissolve.

A still, then a 10 s test, then one sample chapter. Stop for approval at each.
