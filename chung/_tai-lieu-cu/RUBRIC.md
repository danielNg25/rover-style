# RUBRIC: scoring a finished shot and a finished sequence

For independent judges who see frame strips, not the design sheet's intentions. No reference video is needed for sections 1-4. Judge what is on screen.

## 1. Evidence pack (the judge must have all of it)
- S1 Shot strip: 6 evenly spaced frames per shot (first, last and 4 between), with the narration clause printed under it.
- S2 Motion pair: first and last frame of each shot overlaid at 50%, to show camera travel and parallax.
- S3 Dense strip: 12 consecutive frames (0.5 s) from the middle of each shot, to show loops, blinks and mouth changes.
- S4 Crops at 100%: the main face, one background region, one text surface if any.
- S5 Sequence strip: one middle frame per shot in order, with shot lengths and the cut time against the anchor word.
- S6 The narration text with word timings for the sequence.
If an item is missing, the criterion that depends on it is scored 1.

## 2. Criteria (score each 1-5; 2 and 4 are in-between)

| # | Criterion | Evidence | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Idea to picture | S1 with clause, S5 | The picture does not show what the clause says, or needs the audio to be understood | The subject is right but the anchor word is not the thing that changes or is centred | With sound off, a viewer could state the clause's point from the strip; the key event lands on the key word |
| C2 | Composition and depth | S1, S2 | Flat line-up; no foreground; subject lost or cut off | Clear subject, 2 depth layers, some dead areas | Subject read in under 1 s; 3+ depth bands; foreground edge; framing matches the shot's purpose and figure-size norm |
| C3 | Set richness and place identity | S1, S4 background crop | Empty or generic backdrop; fewer than 3 prop kinds in a wide | Place is readable; props present but repetitive or evenly scattered | Place, era and owner read in 1 s from a landmark; wide shows 6+ prop kinds; one motivated light source with halo |
| C4 | Character appeal and consistency | S4 face crop, S5 | Off-model between shots, muddy against the set, or unreadable silhouette | On-model; readable; expression set is narrow | Recognisable by silhouette and colour at small size, including from behind; 20-30% lighter than the set; same design in every shot |
| C5 | Acting | S1, S3 | Puppet holds one pose and face for the whole shot | One change per shot; timing unrelated to the words | Changes at 1 per 1.5 s or better; flips land on words; listener reacts separately from the speaker; idle visible on all puppets |
| C6 | Camera | S2 | Static frame, or a move that fights the subject (whip, zoom, rotation) | Drift present but uniform across shots regardless of purpose | Move already running on frame 1 through the cut; 1-3%/s; parallax visible; holds only where the picture itself changes |
| C7 | Liveness and density | S3, S1 | Any frozen stretch over 1.5 s; fewer than 3 movers | Meets the mover floor but loops are obviously cyclic or all in one layer | Meets the floors for its shot type (wide 5 movers and 3 ambient families; medium 4 and 2; close 3 and 1; insert 3 and 1; abstract 5 and 4) across depth layers; nothing draws attention to itself |
| C8 | Finish | S4 crops | Aliased edges, banding, outlines, inconsistent shading, unreadable or misspelt text | Clean, with one visible flaw (flat lighting, missing halo, blur misuse) | Flat 2-3 tone shading, no outlines, halos on emitters, background blur in closes, consistent grain, text correct with full diacritics |
| C9 | Originality | S1, S5, banned-motif list | Contains a banned motif or could be mistaken for the reference channel at thumbnail size | No banned motif, but devices are one-to-one swaps of well-known ones | Own identity kit throughout and at least one device with no counterpart in the reference |
| C10 | Continuity and rhythm (sequence only) | S5, S6 | Screen direction flips, set state jumps unexplained, three or more same-framing shots on one set, or cuts after the anchor word | Continuity holds; framing alternation is mechanical; no reaction or button | Framing changes at each cut; fact-face-consequence used; one new variable per redress; the paragraph ends on a button; shot lengths track clause lengths at 24-28 per minute |

Notes for judges
- C1 is judged before reading the design sheet. Write down what you think the clause is, then compare.
- C7 counts each ambient family once and each puppet once; the camera counts as one mover.
- C9 is judged against the banned-motif list in STYLE-BIBLE.md section 1.3, not against memory of the reference.

## 3. Automatic fails (score is void; the shot or sequence is returned)
Per shot:
1. Static camera with a static puppet, or any frozen sprite.
2. A wide with fewer than 3 ambient families.
3. A close-up of a named character with no face change.
4. Contact fighting shown blow by blow, or a full-body profile walk longer than 1 s.
5. A shake with no impact.
6. A banned motif, a third-party IP character, or an asset from the quarantined recreated-reference folders.
7. Floating caption text, a misspelt word or missing Vietnamese diacritics.
8. An image unchanged for more than 3 s (except a declared final shot).

Per sequence:
9. A cut that lands after its anchor word in more than 10% of shots.
10. Three or more consecutive shots that match a reference run in set type and framing order.
11. Reuse plus redress below 60% of shots in a sequence of 20 or more shots.
12. Metaphor above 15% of shots.

## 4. Combining scores
Shot score (C1-C9), weighted mean on the 1-5 scale:
- C1 weight 2; C5 weight 1.5; C7 weight 1.5; all others weight 1. Total weight 11.
- Pass: weighted mean at least 3.6, no criterion below 3, C1 at least 4, C9 at least 3.
- "Reference level": weighted mean at least 4.3 with no criterion below 4.

Sequence score:
- 0.7 x the mean of its shot scores + 0.3 x C10.
- Pass: at least 3.7, C10 at least 3, and no more than 10% of shots individually failing.

Judges: at least 2 independent judges per item. If two judges differ by 2 or more on any criterion, a third judge scores that criterion and the median is used. Each judge writes one sentence of evidence per criterion, naming the strip and frame.

The pass marks (3.6, 3.7) are to calibrate on the first episode: score three approved shots and three rejected shots and move the mark to separate them.

## 5. Blind comparison protocol (original next to reference, on quality, not similarity)
Purpose: check that an original sequence reaches the reference's level of finish. Looking like the reference is not rewarded; it is penalised.

1. Selection. Take one original sequence and one reference sequence of similar length (20-40 s) and similar shot-type mix (same share of wides, closes and inserts within 10 points). The topics must differ. The reference sequence must not come from the held-out segment unless this is the held-out test.
2. Preparation. A preparer who will not judge builds evidence packs S1-S5 for both in the same format, scales both to the same resolution, removes logos, watermarks, labels' wording (blur text content but keep its presence) and narration text, and labels them X and Y at random.
3. Judging. Three judges who have not seen the design sheets score X and Y separately on C2-C8 and C10. C1 is scored with the clause text restored but translated to a neutral paraphrase. C9 is not scored blind.
4. Per-criterion preference. For each criterion the judge also states "X better", "Y better" or "no difference", with one sentence of evidence.
5. Unblinding and result. The original is at reference level if, across judges, its mean on C2-C8 and C10 is within 0.3 of the reference's mean and it is not rated worse on more than 2 criteria by a majority of judges.
6. Similarity check (separate judge, unblinded). This judge sees both sequences and answers three yes/no questions: could a still from the original be mistaken for the reference channel at thumbnail size; does any run of 3 shots share set type and framing order; does any banned motif appear. Any "yes" fails the original regardless of quality scores.
7. Held-out test. When the original was designed from the held-out narration, also compare norms only: shots per minute, share of reused sets, framing mix, and liveness floors met. High pictorial similarity to the reference's version counts as a fail under step 6.
8. Record. Store scores, preferences and evidence sentences with the episode; use the three lowest criteria as the next iteration's targets.
